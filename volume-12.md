# Symbolic Mechanics — Volume XII: Boundary Parameters and the Mechanics of Intimacy

**Volume:** XII  
**System Role:** Boundary-parameter and intimacy-entry layer  
**Status:** Canonical  

## Research Hook

Most theories describe intimacy in emotional, attachment-based, or personality terms, but do not specify the fixed boundary parameters that determine whether intimacy can enter at all.

Volume XII formalizes that missing layer by modelling intimacy not as feeling, preference, or psychological readiness, but as a boundary outcome computed from three steady-state parameters: **Visibility (V)**, **Spotlight sensitivity (S)**, and **Alarm threshold**.

Rather than treating closeness as choice, warmth, or emotional resonance, this volume identifies the structural conditions that determine whether relational input can cross the boundary without destabilizing the room.

## Overview

This volume defines the boundary-parameter layer of Symbolic Mechanics.

If Volume XI formalizes intimacy as a boundary event produced by the interaction of V, S, and Δ, then Volume XII explains where these parameters come from, how they become fixed, and how they determine adult entry conditions. In system terms, this volume moves from intimacy configuration to intimacy parameterization.

The volume argues that parents do not generate personality. They generate **boundary parameters**. The system first acquires a readable force map during paternal delayed-entry, then finalizes parameter values during adolescent recalibration. From these stages, the room computes baseline **Visibility (V)**, **Spotlight sensitivity (S)**, and **Alarm threshold**.

Its central modelling objects are force distribution, parameter initialization, adolescent recalibration, steady-state boundary computation, and the formal condition under which intimacy becomes mechanically admissible. Adult intimacy is treated not as preference, but as a gate outcome executed on fixed V × S × Alarm parameters.

## Why this volume matters

This volume is the first major parameterization layer of the intimacy stack.

It matters because a deterministic architecture cannot explain intimacy fully if it only models opening events. It must also specify the parameter architecture that makes opening possible, restrictive, delayed, or impossible under different structural conditions. Volume XII therefore supplies the missing bridge between early force-distribution and adult intimacy behaviour.

Architecturally, this volume sits downstream of Volume XI and upstream of later parental boundary typologies, entry-pattern recognition, and projection-admission mechanics. It explains why two systems can both “want” intimacy yet still differ completely in whether the boundary permits entry.

Without Volume XII, the system can describe intimacy as an event.  
With Volume XII, the system can explain how intimacy thresholds are fixed, why entry conditions differ across configurations, and why chemistry is actually the recognition of a mechanically admissible differential.
