# Symbolic Mechanics — Volume VII: Attraction Tension Mechanics

**Series:** Symbolic Mechanics  
**Volume:** VII  
**Version:** v0.1  
**Architectural Role:** Attraction-tension and dual-vector conflict layer  
**Status:** Canonical  

## Research Hook

Many theories describe attraction as desire, preference, chemistry, attachment style, or emotional pull, but do not specify the mechanical condition under which attraction becomes internally unstable, self-amplifying, and structurally conflictual.

Volume VII formalizes that missing layer. It rewrites attraction not as simple liking or emotional inclination, but as a tension-bearing symbolic configuration in which multiple internal vectors converge on the same object while carrying incompatible loads, projections, and directional demands.

Rather than treating attraction as mood or relationship preference, this volume identifies the mechanics by which attraction becomes a site of internal pressure, oscillation, and destabilizing symbolic strain.

## Overview

This volume defines the attraction-tension layer of Symbolic Mechanics.

If Volume VI explains how symbolic visibility collapses and existence-compensation begins when weight becomes unreadable, then Volume VII explains what happens when symbolic investment converges around an attracting object under conditions of unresolved internal conflict. In system terms, this volume moves from visibility-failure and compensatory orientation toward object-centered tension mechanics.

The volume rewrites attraction as a structured field condition in which symbolic charge, directional pull, internal mismatch, and unresolved pressure coexist inside one relational target. What is often described elsewhere as desire, obsession, longing, or ambivalence is here reformulated as attraction-bearing tension inside a deterministic symbolic system.

Its central modelling objects are attraction vectors, dual-seat or multi-seat convergence, tension accumulation around the target object, and the instability that arises when symbolic pull exceeds coherent internal integration.

## Why this volume matters

This volume is the first major attraction-mechanics layer of the system.

It matters because a deterministic architecture cannot explain attraction adequately if it only models load, collapse, visibility failure, and recovery. It must also specify how an attracting object becomes a tension-bearing symbolic node rather than a neutral relational preference. Volume VII therefore supplies the missing bridge between internal burden and attraction-driven internal conflict.

Architecturally, this volume sits downstream of encoding, load formation, collapse vulnerability, regenerative continuity, boundary regulation, and visibility failure, and upstream of later modules involving instruction resistance, blackout fixation, voluntary shutdown, projection distortion, intimacy mechanics, and relational approach conflict. It provides the first formal account of why attraction itself can become mechanically unstable.

Without Volume VII, the system can describe burden and compensation.  
With Volume VII, the system can explain why attraction generates strain, why pull becomes conflict, and why the object of desire becomes a site of symbolic tension rather than simple preference.
