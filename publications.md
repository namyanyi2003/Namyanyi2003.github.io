## Why this page matters

These publications do not present isolated themes. Together, they define the core runtime of the Symbolic Mechanics engine: how symbolic input is encoded, how pressure accumulates, how rupture and restart occur, how boundaries open or fail, and how the system behaves when visibility, control, or coherence break down.

## Volumes I–X

These ten volumes establish the core runtime architecture of Symbolic Mechanics: kernel processing, symbolic encoding, collapse-capable load formation, regenerative restart, boundary geometry, visibility failure, attraction fields, intrusion physics, blackout topology, and voluntary shutdown. Read in sequence, they define how the room forms, destabilizes, protects itself, loses readability, and re-routes under pressure.

- [Volume I — Canonical Engine Kernel](./volume-01.md)  
  **Kernel layer**  
  Most theories describe inner life, but do not specify the irreducible engine through which symbolic events must pass. Volume I formalizes that missing kernel by defining the deterministic loop **Δ → S → L → R → Exit**.

- [Volume II — Structural Encoding, Symbolic Weight, and Base-Vector Geometry](./volume-02.md)

  Most theories describe feeling, memory, or interpretation, but do not specify how symbolic material becomes fixed into durable structure. Volume II formalizes that missing encoding layer by modelling structural inscription, symbolic weight, and base-vector formation inside the early seat economy.

- [Volume III — Shadow-Load Formation and Collapse Dynamics](./volume-03.md)  
  **Load-formation and collapse layer**  
  Most theories describe repression, distress, or breakdown, but do not specify how unresolved symbolic material becomes accumulating internal pressure. Volume III formalizes that missing collapse layer by modelling shadow-load as the bridge between encoded structure and rupture-capable mechanics.

- [Volume IV — Symbolic Parents, Companion Emergence, and Low-Energy Exit](./volume-04.md)  
  **Companion-module and regenerative-exit layer**  
  Most theories describe support or healing in emotional terms, but do not specify the structural condition under which a symbolic system gains a lower-energy route of continuation. Volume IV formalizes that missing stabilizer layer by modelling symbolic parents as functional channels, defining parental-volume growth, and showing how the C-Module enables low-energy regenerative exit.

- [Volume V — Change Failure, Safe-Proximity Radius, and the Geometry of Counterforce](./volume-05.md)  
  **Boundary-geometry and change-permission layer**  
  Most theories explain failed change in motivational or emotional terms, but do not specify the boundary geometry that makes movement mechanically impossible even when intention is present. - [Volume VI — Visibility Collapse, Sorting Failure, and Existence Compensation](./volume-06.md)

- [Volume IX — Black Room Collapse, the Orb, and Delayed Shame After Reconnection](./volume-09.md)  
  **Blackout-state and attractor-governance layer**  
  Most theories describe compulsion, fixation, or self-loss through desire, reward, or habit, but do not specify the blackout-state in which continuation becomes mechanically inevitable. - [Volume X — Voluntary Boundary Shutdown, Existential Supplementation, and Delayed Reconvergence](./volume-10.md)
